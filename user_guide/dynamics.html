Dynamics#

Last revised by G. Fereoli on 2026 OCT 11

Three objects define the dynamics. A StateArray holds what is propagated and estimated. A ForceModelTranslation selects the accelerations. A Propagator integrates the state together with its state transition matrix (STM), which the filters need.

The state#

A StateDefinition is built by chaining components and is then attached to an epoch and an origin:

eta0 = scb.ArrayWFrame(scb.ArrayWUnits(np.array([1.0]), None), None)
state = (scb.StateDefinition()
         .position(sc, r0)
         .velocity(sc, v0)
         .param("eta_srp", sc, eta0, dynamics="static"))
x0 = scb.StateArray(epoch=t0, origin=earth, state=state)

Every component takes two modes:

  • estimation: "estimated" (corrected by the filter) or "considered" (enters the covariance only).

  • dynamics: "dynamic" (propagated) or "static" (constant over the arc).

Dynamic components come before static ones, and estimated components come before considered ones. Each .velocity() immediately follows its .position(). The StateDefinition docstring lists every allowed component name. Examples are SRP and thermal scale factors, maneuver Δv, finite-burn angles, empirical accelerations, gravity coefficients, measurement biases, and station and Earth orientation offsets.

Force models#

ForceModelTranslation takes the spacecraft and one flag per model. The point mass of the propagation origin is always included, unless polyhedral gravity replaces it. Call list_forces() to print the active models.

Group

Flags

Gravity

third_bodies (list of CelestialBody); sph_harm with sph_harm_body/sph_harm_order/sph_harm_cs_file, or sph_harm_bodies for several fields (one dict per body); polyhedral_gravity with polyhedron_shape and polyhedron_density or polyhedron_gm

Radiation

cannonball_SRP (scaled by eta_srp); nplate_SRP (takes precedence); thermal_radiation; antenna_radiation_pressure; yarkovsky_effect

Maneuvers

maneuver (impulsive or finite); smoothed_maneuvers; finite_burn

Empirical

first_order_gauss_markov, piecewise_first_order_gauss_markov, fourier_acceleration, piecewise_polynomial. Each has a *_frame option: "inertial", "RTN" or "body_fixed"

User-defined

custom_models: a list of CustomDynamicModel. Their CustomParameter objects are estimated like built-in ones

 
fm = scb.ForceModelTranslation(
    primary_body=sc,
    third_bodies=scb.CelestialBody.from_constants(["SUN", "MOON"]),
    cannonball_SRP=True,
    sph_harm=True,
    sph_harm_bodies=[{"body": earth, "order": 8, "cs_file": egm_file}],
)
print(fm.list_forces())

The spherical-harmonic, n-plate SRP and polyhedron accelerations run in the Rust extension. Third-body and Sun positions come from an ephemeris cache that is built once per propagation instead of being queried from SPICE at every step. Rotational dynamics are not modelled yet: body attitude comes from SPICE frames or from the attitude laws of the n-plate model.

Propagation and the STM#

The propagator integrates \(\dot{X} = f(X, t)\) and, by default, the variational equations

\[\dot{\Phi}(t, t_0) = A(t)\,\Phi(t, t_0), \qquad A(t) = \frac{\partial f}{\partial X}, \qquad \Phi(t_0, t_0) = I .\]

Static parameters have no dynamics of their own, but their STM columns record how they move the dynamic states.

prop = scb.Propagator(primary_body=sc, state_vector=x0, tspan=epochs,
                      force_models=fm, integrator="DOP853")
prop.propagate()

traj = scb.Trajectory(state_array=prop.propagated_state_array)
traj.write_to_spk("orbiter.bsp")

  • integrator: "DOP853" (default, tolerances atol/rtol = 2.2e-14) or "IAS15" (Rust). IAS15 does not propagate the STM yet, so it needs propagate_STM=False.

  • tspan: an EpochArray sets the output grid. A (t0, tf) tuple outputs on the integrator’s own steps, resampled n_densifier times.

  • write_to_spk writes the SPK plus JSON sidecars with the STMs, the parameters and the propagator settings. The measurement models and filters read these sidecars. Each filter iteration writes its own reference SPK, named by traj_name and traj_dir.

Mission sequences#

A MissionSequence chains legs that each have their own propagator and force model. Impulsive burns and zero-duration nodes join the legs.

mission = scb.MissionSequence(name="Mission")
mission.addLeg("Cruise", prop=prop1)
mission.addBurn("TCM-1", dV=dv, prop=prop1)
mission.addLeg("Approach", prop=prop2)
scenario = mission.propagate()

traj = scb.Trajectory(leg_array=scenario)

addFiniteBurn adds a thrust arc, and addNode adds an event marker. An estimated impulsive burn needs a dv_man parameter in the next leg’s state. A MissionSequence can be passed to any filter in place of a single propagator.
