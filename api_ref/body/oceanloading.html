OceanLoading#

class OceanLoading(amplitudes: tuple, phases: tuple, rate_step: float = 60.0)#

Bases: object

Ocean-loading displacement of a station, from BLQ coefficients.

Ocean loading is the crust’s response to the weight of the ocean tide. The tide moves a large mass of water over the crust twice a day, and the crust sags under it. A coastal station moves by up to a few centimetres, and an inland one by a few millimetres. The response depends on the station and the ocean model, so it is given per station as the amplitudes and phases of a BLQ table, such as those from the Onsala loading service (http://holt.oso.chalmers.se/loading/).

For each constituent \(i\) with astronomical argument \(\theta_i + \chi_i\) (see OceanLoadingInfo) and nodal corrections \(f_i, u_i\), the displacement in m is (Moyer, with the nodal corrections of IERS Conventions (2010), eq. 7.16):

\[\begin{split}\Delta z &= \sum_i f_i A^r_i \cos(\theta_i + \chi_i + u_i - \phi^r_i) \\ \Delta n &= -\sum_i f_i A^S_i \cos(\theta_i + \chi_i + u_i - \phi^S_i) \\ \Delta e &= -\sum_i f_i A^W_i \cos(\theta_i + \chi_i + u_i - \phi^W_i)\end{split}\]

Here \(A\) and \(\phi\) are the BLQ amplitudes and Greenwich phase lags of the radial, south and west components.

Parameters:
amplitudesarray_like, shape (3, 11)

Amplitudes, m. The rows are radial, west and south, and the columns are the constituents of Constants.EARTH_OCEAN_LOADING, in the order of a BLQ table.

phasesarray_like, shape (3, 11)

Greenwich phase lags, degrees, in the same layout.

rate_stepfloat, optional

Half-interval in seconds for the central difference that supplies the body-fixed rate, default 60.

Notes

The nodal corrections \(f\) and \(u\) follow the 18.6-year regression of the lunar node (nodal_corrections()). Moyer leaves them out; they scale the lunar terms by up to 4% for M2 and 40% for Mf.

The Doodson variable \(\tau\) needs UT1. When the station’s Earth-fixed frame is given it is read from that frame’s rotation, so the phase follows the same Earth rotation that places the station (see arguments()); without a frame UTC stands in for UT1, which moves the phase by at most a few millidegrees.

References

Moyer, T. D., Formulation for Observed and Computed Values of Deep Space Network Data Types for Navigation, JPL Publication 00-7, 2000.

IERS Conventions (2010), IERS Technical Note 36, Section 7.1.2, eq. 7.16.

static earth_rotation_angle(epoch: float, frame: str) → float#

Earth rotation angle of an Earth-fixed frame, radians.

Parameters:
epochfloat

TDB seconds past J2000.

framestr

Earth-fixed frame, e.g. "ITRF93".

Returns:
float

Angle from the celestial to the terrestrial intermediate origin, in \((-\pi, \pi]\).

Notes

With \(M\) the J2000-to-frame rotation, the third row of \(M\) is the pole \((X, Y, Z)\) in J2000. The CIO-based matrix \(Q = R_3(-E) R_2(-d) R_3(E) R_3(s)\) (IERS 2010 eq. 5.6) with \(E = \mathrm{atan2}(Y, X)\), \(d = \arccos Z\) (eq. 5.7) and \(s \approx -XY/2\), the leading term of eq. 5.9, leaves \(M Q = R_3(\theta)\) up to the polar motion, whose effect on \(\theta\) is second order. Through the IERS definition \(\theta = 2\pi(0.7790572732640 + 1.00273781191135448\,D_U)\) (eq. 5.14), with \(D_U\) the UT1 Julian date minus 2451545.0, this angle is UT1: for ITRF93 it recovers the tabulated UT1 - UTC to tens of microseconds.

References

IERS Conventions (2010), IERS Technical Note 36, Section 5.4.4, eqs. 5.6, 5.7 and 5.9, and Section 5.5.3, eq. 5.14.

static nodal_corrections(epoch: float) → tuple#

Nodal factors \(f_i\) and angles \(u_i\) of the constituents.

Parameters:
epochfloat

TDB seconds past J2000.

Returns:
tuple of numpy.ndarray

(f, u): the amplitude factors, and the phase corrections in degrees, one per constituent of Constants.EARTH_OCEAN_LOADING.

Notes

\(f = \sum_k a_k \cos kN\) and \(u = \sum_k b_k \sin kN\), with \(N\) the longitude of the Moon’s ascending node, the Delaunay \(\Omega\).

References

Pugh, D. T., Tides, Surges and Mean Sea-Level, Wiley, 1987, Table 4.3, after Schureman, P., Manual of Harmonic Analysis and Prediction of Tides, US Coast and Geodetic Survey Special Publication 98, 1958.
