GroundStation#

class GroundStation(name: str, spice_id: int | str = None, *, central_body='EARTH', body_fixed_frame='ITRF93', body_fixed_position=None, body_fixed_velocity=None, position_epoch=None, surface_displacement=None, barycentric_transformation=False, body_orientation=None)#

Bases: Body

Represents a ground station for spacecraft communication modeling.

A ground station is a Body with no mass or SRP properties. Its primary role is to supply the observer position for radiometric observables (range, range-rate, Doppler).

Parameters:
namestr

The name of the ground station.

spice_idint or float, optional

The SPICE ID of the ground station, defaults to None.

central_bodystr or Body, optional

Body supporting the station, default EARTH. The supplied coordinates or station kernels express its position relative to this center.

body_fixed_frameFrame or str, optional

Body-fixed SPICE frame. For another planet supply its frame, e.g. IAU_MARS, together with central_body="MARS". Defaults to 'ITRF93'.

body_fixed_positionArrayWUnits or array_like, optional

Fixed Cartesian station coordinates relative to central_body in body_fixed_frame. Bare values are km. When supplied, no station SPK is needed; otherwise the station position comes from loaded kernels. Use from_cylindrical() for a longitude/radius/Z table.

body_fixed_velocityArrayWUnits or array_like, optional

Constant body-fixed velocity of explicit coordinates, plate motion. Bare values are km/s. Requires position_epoch.

position_epochEpochArray or float, optional

Single epoch at which body_fixed_position holds; a float is TDB seconds past J2000. Defaults to None.

surface_displacementobject or list of objects, optional

Time-varying station displacement, e.g. SolidTide, PoleTide, OceanLoading, or a list of them, summed. See surface_displacement.

barycentric_transformationbool, optional

Apply the geocentric-to-barycentric transformation to space-fixed states, default false. See barycentric_transformation.

body_orientationBodyOrientation, optional

Small orientation correction to the loaded body-fixed frame, from earth_correction(). Absolute EOPs are loaded as a PCK instead. See body_orientation.

iau_pole_correction(epoch, frame='J2000', **pole) → ArrayWFrame#

State correction from an estimated IAU pole and prime meridian.

Parameters:
epochEpochArray or float

Single epoch; a float is TDB seconds past J2000.

frameFrame or str, optional

Output axes. Defaults to 'J2000'.

**poleArrayWUnits or float

Estimated pole_ra, pole_dec, pole_w0 and pole_pm of the central body, as iau_angles() takes them. Bare numbers are radians and, for pole_pm, deg/day.

Returns:
ArrayWFrame

Six components in km and km/s in frame, to add to nominal_state().

Raises:
MissingPoleCoefficientsError

From iau_angles(), raised by iau_pole_coefficients() reading no secular constants for the central body.

Notes

The estimate is applied as a perturbation of the station’s own frame: with \(R_e\) and \(R_s\) the body-fixed to J2000 rotations of iau_angles() with and without the estimated values, the station moves to \(r' = P r\) with \(P = R_e R_s^T\), and

\[v' = \dot P r + P v, \qquad \dot P = [\omega_e \times] P - P [\omega_s \times],\]

where \(r, v\) are nominal_state() in J2000 and \(\omega\) the body’s angular velocity. \(P\) is exact, not linearized, and is the identity whenever the estimate equals the secular model; for a body whose PCK frame has no nutation terms (Mars in pck00010) the station then turns with exactly the orientation that SphericalHarmonicsGravity and the optical models apply for the same state. An estimated pole_ra or pole_dec is constant and pole_pm drops the quadratic term, as there, so an estimate held at the PCK constant term drifts from the secular model by the dropped rate over the years since J2000.

The perturbation acts on nominal_state(), not on the estimated location or body-fixed corrections; those cross terms are products of two corrections.

iau_pole_partials(epoch, frame='J2000', **pole) → ArrayWUnits#

Sensitivity of this station’s state to the estimated IAU pole.

Parameters:
epochEpochArray or float

Single epoch; a float is TDB seconds past J2000.

frameFrame or str, optional

Output axes. Defaults to 'J2000'.

**poleArrayWUnits or float

Values to linearize about, as in iau_pole_correction(); a missing one is linearized about its secular term.

Returns:
ArrayWUnits

(6, 4) Jacobian of iau_pole_correction() in frame axes, columns pole_ra, pole_dec, pole_pm, pole_w0 (the order of BodyOrientation.IAU_POLE_PARAMETERS), in km and km/s per radian (ROTATION_PARTIAL_UNITS) and, for pole_pm, per deg/day (POLE_PM_PARTIAL_UNITS).

Raises:
MissingPoleCoefficientsError

As in iau_pole_correction().

Notes

Each angle turns the estimated orientation about one J2000 axis \(e\) of _iau_axes(), so with \(g\) the derivative of the angle with respect to the parameter (1, or \(d\,\pi/180\) for pole_pm with \(d\) days past J2000),

\[\frac{\partial r'}{\partial q} = g\, e \times r', \qquad \frac{\partial v'}{\partial q} = \dot g\, e \times r' + g\,(\dot e \times r' + e \times v'),\]

where \(\dot e\) follows the estimated orientation: \(\dot{\hat z} = 0\), \(-\dot{\hat n} = \dot\alpha_e (\cos\alpha_e, \sin\alpha_e, 0)\) and \(\dot{\hat p} = \omega_e \times \hat p\).

inertial_rotation_partials(epoch, frame='J2000') → ArrayWUnits#

Sensitivity of this station’s state to a J2000 rotation vector.

Parameters:
epochEpochArray or float

Single epoch; a float is TDB seconds past J2000.

frameFrame or str, optional

Output axes. Defaults to 'J2000'.

Returns:
ArrayWUnits

(6, 3) Jacobian in km and km/s per radian (ROTATION_PARTIAL_UNITS), in frame axes. The correction is linear in the rotation, so the Jacobian times the rotation vector is also the state correction itself.

Notes

A small rotation \(\omega\) held fixed in inertial space moves the station by \(\omega \times r_I\) and its inertial velocity by \(\omega \times v_I\), so the derivative is \(-[r_I\times]\) and \(-[v_I\times]\). This differs from rotation_partials(), whose rotation is held fixed in the body and so turns with it. A celestial-pole offset is of this inertial kind.

The rotation acts on nominal_state(), not on the estimated location or body-fixed corrections; those cross terms are products of two small corrections.

pole_tide_partials(epoch, frame='J2000') → ArrayWUnits#

Sensitivity of this station’s state to the pole the pole tide uses.

Parameters:
epochEpochArray or float

Single epoch; a float is TDB seconds past J2000.

frameFrame or str, optional

Output axes. Defaults to 'J2000'.

Returns:
ArrayWUnits

(6, 2) Jacobian in km and km/s per radian of pole x and y (ROTATION_PARTIAL_UNITS), in frame axes, or zeros when no model with state_displacement_pole_partials, such as a PoleTide, is attached.

Notes

An estimated eop_delta_pole_x/eop_delta_pole_y turns the station through rotation_partials() and also moves the pole the pole tide responds to. The tide is linear in the pole, so this Jacobian times the pole delta is the exact change, taken about the undisplaced position like tide_partials().

reset_mass_history() → None#

Restore the mass the body was built with and forget fitted profiles.

Every propagated leg appends the profile it fitted, and the next leg starts from the last one. Propagating the same mission again (a filter re-linearizing its reference, say) would otherwise start the first leg from the mass the previous run ended with, and get_mass() would return profiles from earlier runs that cover the same epochs.

The profile lists are cleared in place: force-model copies of this body share them by reference.

Returns:
None

ValueError

For non-finite corrections, undefined cylindrical longitude, or an attached orientation expressed in a different body-fixed frame.

Notes

Location is applied before the first-order rigid rotation, retaining the location/orientation cross term. The full sxform carries both the rotated displacement and its velocity. This method uses nominal kernels directly and does not change their frame definitions.

station_visibility(target: Spacecraft, epoch: EpochArray, elevation_mask: float = 10.0, return_angles: bool = False, frame: str = 'J2000', origin: str = 'EARTH')#

Determines if a spacecraft is visible to the ground station given an elevation mask for a single epoch or a sequence of epochs.

Positions are retrieved via SPICE using the SPICE names/IDs of this ground station and the target spacecraft, then the elevation (and optionally azimuth) are computed in the requested reference frame.

The station elevation angle \(\varepsilon\) above the local horizon is computed from

\[\varepsilon = 90^\circ - \theta_{nadir}, \qquad \cos\theta_{nadir} = \frac{(\mathbf{r}_{sc} - \mathbf{r}_{gs})\cdot\mathbf{r}_{gs}} {\|\mathbf{r}_{sc} - \mathbf{r}_{gs}\|\,\|\mathbf{r}_{gs}\|}\]

The station is visible when \(\varepsilon \geq \varepsilon_{mask}\) (default 10°).

Parameters:
targetSpacecraft

The target spacecraft. Its SPICE ID is used to query its state via SPICE.

epochEpochArray

The epoch or sequence of epochs at which to evaluate visibility.

elevation_maskfloat, optional

Minimum elevation angle in degrees above the horizon for the spacecraft to be considered visible. Defaults to 10.0.

return_anglesbool, optional

If True, also returns the elevation and azimuth angles. Defaults to False.

framestr, optional

Reference frame for position queries. Defaults to 'J2000'.

originstr, optional

Observer body for SPICE state queries. Defaults to 'EARTH'.

Returns:
visiblebool or np.ndarray of bool

True if the spacecraft is above the elevation mask as seen from this ground station. Returns a scalar for a single epoch or an array for multiple epochs.

elevationfloat or np.ndarray of float

Elevation angle(s) in degrees above the horizon. Only returned if return_angles = True.

azimuthfloat or np.ndarray of float

Azimuth angle(s) in degrees measured clockwise from North. Only returned if return_angles = True.

tide_correction(epoch, frame='J2000', *, love_h2=None, shida_l2=None) → ArrayWFrame#

Move the attached tide from its own Love numbers to estimated ones.

Parameters:
epochEpochArray or float

Single epoch; a float is TDB seconds past J2000.

frameFrame or str, optional

Output axes. Defaults to 'J2000'.

love_h2, shida_l2float, optional

Estimated \(h_2\) and \(l_2\), read as the numbers of tide_reference. Defaults to None, meaning each attached model keeps its own number.

Returns:
ArrayWFrame

State delta in km and km/s in frame, shape (6,); zeros when no tide model with Love-number partials is attached.

Notes

nominal_state() already carries each tide model at its own \(h_2\) and \(l_2\), so only the difference is added here. Every attached model shifts by the same difference, the estimate minus the tide_reference number: a solid tide and a pole tide keep their different nominal numbers and move together, so tide_partials() is their sum. The degree-2 displacement is linear in both numbers, which makes that sum times the difference exact for the closed-form models and first order for the IERS 2010 routine.

tide_partials(epoch, frame='J2000') → ArrayWUnits#

Sensitivity of this station’s state to the Love and Shida numbers.

Parameters:
epochEpochArray or float

Single epoch; a float is TDB seconds past J2000.

frameFrame or str, optional

Output axes. Defaults to 'J2000'.

Returns:
ArrayWUnits

(6, 2) Jacobian in km and km/s (STATE_UNITS) per unit \(h_2\) or \(l_2\), in frame axes, or zeros when no surface_displacement is attached.

Notes

The tidal response is what these numbers scale, so a station with no tide model attached has no sensitivity to them – estimating them then gives a zero column, which a filter will not like. The Jacobian is taken about the undisplaced position, which is where the tide model itself evaluates. It sums every attached model, since tide_correction() shifts all of them by the same amount.

property barycentric_transformation: bool#

Whether space-fixed states carry the geocentric-to-barycentric transformation.

Station coordinates are geocentric and in terrestrial units; a spacecraft trajectory and the planetary ephemerides are barycentric, in TDB units. Combining them consistently takes the Lorentz transformation of the station’s geocentric space-fixed position into the solar-system barycentric frame (Moyer, Formulation for Observed and Computed Values of Deep Space Network Data Types for Navigation, 2000, eq. 4-10):

\[\mathbf r_B = \left(1 - \tilde L - \frac{\gamma U}{c^2}\right)\mathbf r - \frac{(\mathbf V \cdot \mathbf r)\,\mathbf V}{2c^2}\]

with \(U\) the Sun’s potential at the central body, \(\mathbf V\) the central body’s barycentric velocity, and \(\tilde L\), \(\gamma\) from constants. The scale term is 2.5e-8, about 16 cm at the Earth’s surface, and the velocity term up to 3 cm on a daily cycle. JPL’s navigation software reports station states this way.

When true, nominal_state() applies it to output in any axes but the station’s body-fixed frame, whose output is unchanged; being a scale and a dot product, it is the same in any axes. Only the position is transformed. The velocity term would be 0.01 mm/s, and the light-time observables’ own station transformation (Measurement._compute_GS_in_SSB_space_time_RLT) is likewise applied to position alone. Defaults to false.

Raises:
TypeError

If set to anything other than a bool.

property body_fixed_frame: str#

Body-fixed SPICE frame name of this station, e.g. ITRF93 or IAU_MARS.

Station coordinates, surface displacements and estimated location and orientation corrections are all expressed in these axes. Assign a Frame or a frame name; the name is stored.

property body_fixed_position#

Explicit Cartesian coordinates in km, or None for a kernel station.

Returns a copy. Assign three finite lengths (ArrayWUnits or km values) to select a fixed table position; assign None to use the station SPK. No station kernel is written, loaded, or modified by this setting.

property body_fixed_velocity#

Constant body-fixed velocity of explicit coordinates in km/s, or None.

Plate motion. Published station coordinates hold at an epoch, position_epoch, and move from there at a published rate: the station is at body_fixed_position + body_fixed_velocity * (t - position_epoch) at every epoch t, with this velocity as its body-fixed rate. Returns a copy. Assign three finite speeds (ArrayWUnits or km/s values), or None for a station that does not move.

property body_orientation#

Optional BodyOrientation station correction.

earth_orientation remains an alias. This is a correction to the loaded nominal frame, not an absolute EOP model or a force-model change.

property earth_orientation#

Earth orientation correction applied to this station, or None.

A body-fixed frame from a binary PCK already carries that kernel’s polar motion and UT1. Setting an BodyOrientation here applies the difference between the kernels’ values and the ones an analysis pins, so the station’s inertial position matches the intended Earth orientation.

property surface_displacement#

Optional time-varying displacement of this station in its body-fixed frame.

Set a SolidTide to move the station with the solid tide. Unlike body_orientation, this is not a correction to a nominal that might instead have been right: the station really is somewhere else at this epoch, so the displacement enters nominal_state() and every estimated parameter is linearised about the displaced point.

Any object exposing state_displacement(position, epoch, frame) and returning six components, as an ArrayWUnits or bare km and km/s, works, such as PoleTide and OceanLoading. The position it receives is bare km. A list or tuple of such models is summed, each evaluated at the undisplaced position. The property returns whatever was set.
