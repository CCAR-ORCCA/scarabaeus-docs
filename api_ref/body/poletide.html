PoleTide#

class PoleTide(pole_x: float, pole_y: float, central_body: str = 'EARTH', love_h2: float = None, shida_l2: float = None)#

Bases: object

Pole-tide displacement of a surface point.

The pole tide is the solid body’s response to the wander of its rotation axis. Polar motion moves the rotation axis relative to the crust, which changes the centrifugal potential felt at the surface. The body deforms in response, like it does under the solid tide, and a station moves by up to about 2.5 cm radially and 7 mm horizontally at Earth.

With \(X, Y\) the pole coordinates, \(\bar X, \bar Y\) the mean pole, \(\phi, \lambda\) the geocentric latitude and longitude of the station at radius \(r\), and \(\omega^2 r^2/g\) with \(g = GM/r^2\), the displacement is (Moyer, with IERS Conventions (2010), eq. 7.26):

\[\begin{split}\Delta r &= -\frac{h_2}{2}\frac{\omega^2 r^2}{g}\sin 2\phi \left[(X - \bar X)\cos\lambda - (Y - \bar Y)\sin\lambda\right] \\ \Delta n &= -l_2\frac{\omega^2 r^2}{g}\cos 2\phi \left[(X - \bar X)\cos\lambda - (Y - \bar Y)\sin\lambda\right] \\ \Delta e &= l_2\frac{\omega^2 r^2}{g}\sin\phi \left[(X - \bar X)\sin\lambda + (Y - \bar Y)\cos\lambda\right]\end{split}\]
Parameters:
pole_x, pole_yArrayWUnits or float

Pole coordinates \(X, Y\); bare numbers are radians, and they are stored as radians. They are the same numbers as pole_x and pole_y.

central_bodystr, optional

Deformed body, default EARTH. Its GM and the rotation rate set the scale. It must be listed in NOMINAL_RESPONSE.

love_h2, shida_l2float, optional

Love and Shida numbers of the response. Default to Constants.EARTH_POLE_TIDE.

Notes

The pole is held constant, so the displacement does not vary in time and its body-fixed rate is zero. At Earth the pole moves a few milliarcseconds a day, a rate of about 1e-12 km/s, which is far below Doppler noise.

The displacement is linear in \(h_2\) and \(l_2\) and, separately, in the pole offset, so state_displacement_partials() and state_displacement_pole_partials() are exact. The second is what lets an estimated eop_delta_pole_x/eop_delta_pole_y move the pole tide along with the station rotation.

The east component takes the sign of IERS eq. 7.26, which is also the sign the centrifugal potential gives. Moyer prints the opposite sign. Using that sign would move the station by at most about 1 mm.

References

Moyer, T. D., Formulation for Observed and Computed Values of Deep Space Network Data Types for Navigation, JPL Publication 00-7, 2000.

IERS Conventions (2010), IERS Technical Note 36, Section 7.1.4.

Parameters:
position_body_fixedArrayWUnits, ArrayWFrame or array_like, shape (3,)

Undisplaced position; see displacement(). Bare numbers are km.

epochfloat, optional

Not used, because the pole is constant. Defaults to None.

Not used. Defaults to None.

Returns:
ArrayWUnits

(6, 2) Jacobian in km and km/s per unit Love or Shida number, columns \(h_2\) and \(l_2\), the same columns as state_displacement_partials(). The rate rows are zero.

Raises:
UndefinedVerticalError

If the position is at the body centre or on the rotation axis.

Notes

\(h_2\) scales only the radial term and \(l_2\) only the two horizontal ones, so each column is the displacement at a unit number and zero for the other: exact, not a linearization.

The displacement is linear in the offset from the mean pole, so each column is the displacement for a unit offset in that coordinate: exact. A GroundStation sums these through pole_tide_partials() so an estimated eop_delta_pole_x/eop_delta_pole_y moves this tide too.
