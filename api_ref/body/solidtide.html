SolidTide#

class SolidTide(tide_bodies: tuple = None, central_body: str = 'EARTH', love_h2: float = None, shida_l2: float = None, love_k: dict = None, remove_permanent_tide: bool = False, rate_step: float = 60.0, model: str = 'degree2')#

Bases: object

Solid tide displacement of a surface point.

A station is not fixed in its body-fixed frame. Nearby masses deform the solid body it stands on, moving it by tens of centimetres on a diurnal and semidiurnal cycle. That is well above the noise of a modern ranging system, so a station geometry that omits it carries a signature no estimated parameter is supposed to absorb.

By default the degree-2 in-phase term of the IERS Conventions (2010), Chapter 7, Section 7.1.1, step 1; model="IERS2010" evaluates the complete IERS routine instead. The degree-2 term is

\[\delta\mathbf r = \sum_j \frac{GM_j}{GM_\oplus} \frac{R^4}{R_j^3} \left\{ h_2 \hat{\mathbf r} \left[\tfrac{3}{2}(\hat{\mathbf R}_j\cdot\hat{\mathbf r})^2 - \tfrac{1}{2}\right] + 3 l_2 (\hat{\mathbf R}_j\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}) \left[\hat{\mathbf R}_j - (\hat{\mathbf R}_j\cdot\hat{\mathbf r})\hat{\mathbf r} \right]\right\}\]

summed over the tide-raising bodies.

One tide model serves two consumers. Attach it to GroundStation.surface_displacement to move a station on the surface, and to SphericalHarmonicsGravity.solid_tide to perturb the gravity field.

Nothing in the model is specific to Earth. Earth is the one body with a nominal response built in, because it is the one whose Love and Shida numbers are pinned by a convention; a station on any other body gets the same treatment once its numbers are supplied.

Parameters:
tide_bodiestuple of str, optional

Tide-raising bodies. Defaults to the Moon and the Sun for Earth, where everything else is below a millimetre. Required for any other central body.

central_bodystr, optional

Deformed body, default EARTH. Its GM and equatorial radius set the scale, and the tide-raiser positions are taken relative to it. Nothing in the formulation is Earth-specific – a station on Mars gets its tide from this same class, given Mars values.

love_h2, shida_l2float, optional

Degree-2 Love and Shida numbers. Default to the IERS nominal values of Constants.EARTH_SOLID_TIDE, 0.6078 and 0.0847, for Earth only. For any other body they are required: the tidal response varies by a factor of several between bodies (Mars is near h2 = 0.17), so a default would be a large silent error rather than an approximation.

love_kdict, optional

Potential Love numbers \(k_{nm}\) keyed by (degree, order), used only by geopotential_coefficients(). Defaults to the IERS Table 6.3 values for Earth, degrees 2 and 3, from Constants.EARTH_SOLID_TIDE. These are a table rather than one number for the same reason the field itself is: they vary with order (\(k_{20}\) and \(k_{22}\) differ by about 1%) and fall by a factor of three from degree 2 to degree 3. Supplying a subset restricts the perturbation to those terms.

These are a different quantity from love_h2: \(h\) says how far the surface moves, \(k\) how much the external gravity field changes. Confusing them is a factor-of-two error.

remove_permanent_tidebool, optional

Subtract the zero-frequency (permanent) deformation, IERS eq. 7.14. Default false, which is what conventional tide-free station coordinates need. See Notes – this is a real fork, not a preference.

rate_stepfloat, optional

Half-interval in seconds for the central difference that supplies the body-fixed rate, default 60.

model{“degree2”, “IERS2010”}, optional

"degree2" (default) is the degree-2 in-phase term above. "IERS2010" is the complete IERS Conventions (2010) routine DEHANTTIDEINEL, Earth only, with its own constants (Constants.EARTH_SOLID_TIDE): step 1 in and out of phase for degrees 2 and 3 with latitude-dependent Love and Shida numbers, and the step-2 frequency corrections. It differs from the degree-2 term by up to about 13 mm, mostly the diurnal K1 correction.

Notes

The degree-2 model retains the degree-2 in-phase term, which is the whole of the effect at the decimetre level – up to about 0.4 m radially at Earth.

The permanent-tide constants are Earth values from IERS eq. 7.14, kept in Constants.EARTH_SOLID_TIDE, so remove_permanent_tide is only meaningful for Earth.

The degree-2 model omits, with magnitudes: degree 3 (about 2 mm), the out-of-phase diurnal and semidiurnal contributions and the latitude dependence of \(h\) and \(l\) (a few mm), and the frequency-dependent step-2 corrections (up to about 13 mm, the diurnal K1 term above all). model="IERS2010" includes all of these. Neither includes ocean and atmospheric loading (about 1 cm and a few mm) or the pole tide (about 1 cm). displacement_partials() is the degree-2 term’s sensitivity to the Love and Shida numbers under either model.

The permanent tide is a genuine convention fork rather than a refinement. The formula above yields the total deformation including a zero-frequency part – roughly -12 cm radially at the poles and +6 cm at the equator. Conventional tide-free coordinates, which is what the ITRF and DSN tables publish, already exclude that permanent deformation, so it must be restored by this formula and remove_permanent_tide stays false. Set it true only for coordinates that are already mean-tide or zero-tide, or the permanent part is applied twice.

The choice is made on the coordinates’ stated convention, not on residuals: on the C2 and C3 deliveries the two settings differ by 0.010 m in the mean range residual and 0.0002 m in its scatter, so the data does not discriminate between them.

See orientation-station-estimation for how the model enters the station geometry and which higher-order terms are left out.

References

IERS Conventions (2010), IERS Technical Note 36, Chapter 7. https://iers-conventions.obspm.fr/content/chapter7/icc7.pdf

Notes

The displacement is linear in \(h_2\) and \(l_2\), so these columns are the two terms evaluated with a unit coefficient and are exact rather than a linearization. The permanent-tide term does not depend on the Love numbers – it is a tabulated IERS correction – so it contributes nothing here whichever way that option is set.

geopotential_coefficients(epoch: float, frame: str = None) → dict#

Tidal perturbation of the body’s spherical-harmonic coefficients.

Parameters:
epochfloat

TDB seconds past J2000.

framestr, optional

Body-fixed axes the tide-raiser positions are taken in. Defaults to None, 'IAU_' plus the central body’s name.

Returns:
dict

(degree, order) mapped to (dC, dS), normalized, for every (degree, order) present in love_k. Add these to the nominal field.

Notes

IERS Conventions (2010), Chapter 6, eq. 6.6, step 1:

\[\Delta\bar C_{nm} - i\,\Delta\bar S_{nm} = \frac{k_{nm}}{2n+1} \sum_j \frac{GM_j}{GM} \left(\frac{R}{r_j}\right)^{n+1} \bar P_{nm}(\sin\Phi_j)\, e^{-im\lambda_j}\]

This is the other half of the same tide that displacement() moves the surface with: the deformed mass changes the external field. It is driven by love_k, not by \(h_2\) and \(l_2\).

Every (degree, order) in love_k is summed, so the resolution follows that table rather than being fixed at degree 2. At Earth this reaches about 5e-9 in \(\Delta\bar C_{20}\), which is 1e-5 of the nominal \(\bar C_{20}\); the degree-3 terms are three orders of magnitude smaller again.

Each \(\Delta\bar C_{nm}\) depends on its own \(k_{nm}\) and no other, so the sensitivity is diagonal – see geopotential_partials().

Frequency-dependent step-2 corrections and the anelastic \(k^{+}\) term are not included; both are well below the step-1 terms retained here.

Beware the base field’s own tide convention. A geopotential published as zero-tide already contains the permanent deformation, so adding this on top counts it twice; a tide-free field does not. The difference is about 1e-8 in \(\bar C_{20}\), comparable to the whole tidal signal, and this method does not know which convention the loaded coefficients use.
